Treeshelf

Books written by people who simply love to write

Treeshelf is a reading room for amateur authors. Upload a story, a poem collection or a full novel, and let readers open it right here in the browser. Readers need no sign-up, and writers need only a free account.

Why Treeshelf exists

Every library, bookshop and online store has room for writers who have already made it. There is far less room for the student who finished a short story at midnight, the retired teacher with a memoir in a drawer, the teenager writing fantasy in the back of an exercise book, or the neighbour whose poems have never been read by anyone outside the family. Their work is often good. Sometimes it is brilliant. Much of the time, it is simply unseen.

Treeshelf was built to close that gap. It is a plain, calm place where unpublished and self-published writing can be shared as easily as sending a file, and where readers can find that writing without searching through social media feeds, group chats or scattered download links. If you can make a PDF or a Word document, you can have readers.

We believe that a first draft deserves a first reader, that a finished manuscript deserves an audience, and that nobody should need a publishing contract before somebody turns the page.

What “amateur” means here

On Treeshelf, amateur is a word of respect. It comes from the Latin amare, to love. An amateur writer is someone who writes because they love it, not because anyone is paying them to. Some of our writers are beginners learning the craft. Others have written for years and never sought a publisher. Both are welcome, and both will find readers who care about the work for what it is.

You do not need a title, a degree, a following or a literary agent. You need a piece of writing you are willing to share.

How readers find books with ease

Finding good amateur writing used to mean luck. You had to know the right person, join the right forum, or stumble on the right link. Treeshelf removes the luck. Everything lives in one place, called Explore, and Explore is built to be browsed.

  • One page of cards. Every book appears as a card with a cover, a title, the author’s name, a genre and a short description. You can judge a book in a few seconds and move on or open it.
  • Search that works the way you think. Type a title, an author’s name or a word from a description and the cards narrow as you type.
  • Genre filters. Looking for romance tonight, or poetry, or a spooky short story? Pick a genre and see only those books.
  • Read in the browser. Press Read and the book opens in a clean reader on the same page. You do not need to download anything, install anything or create an account.
  • Keep a copy if you like. Prefer to read offline? Every uploaded book has a download button.

A reading experience that is kind to your eyes

Long reading needs a calm page. That is why the whole site sits on a soft, pale background instead of stark white, with dark ink-coloured text, generous spacing between lines and a comfortable line length. When you open a story or a Word document, you can make the text larger or smaller with a single tap. PDFs open in their original layout, exactly as the author designed them, so a poem with careful line breaks or a book with illustrations looks the way it was meant to.

The reader works the same way on a phone, a tablet and a laptop. If you read on the bus, in bed or between lectures, the page will fit your screen.

How writers share their work

Sharing a book takes about a minute. Open the Write page, give your book a title and your name or pen name, choose a genre and write a short description that makes people curious. Then either attach a file or type your story straight into the text box. When you press Publish, your book joins the cards on Explore and anyone can open it.

  • Upload a file. PDF, Word (.docx) and plain text files are all supported. Use PDF when layout matters, and Word or text when you want readers to be able to resize the text.
  • Or write it here. No finished file yet? Paste or type your chapter into the box. It will be shown to readers as a clean, readable page.
  • Add a cover. Upload a cover image and it will appear on your book’s card on Explore. It is resized automatically, so a photo straight from your phone is fine.
  • Publish a novel chapter by chapter. Tick the chapter option when you publish, then add chapters whenever they are ready. Readers can jump between chapters, and you can edit or reorder them at any time.
  • Choose how you appear. Use your real name or a pen name. It is your decision.
  • Add as many books as you like. One poem, a short story series or a twenty-chapter novel. There is no limit on how much you can share.

You can also upload books straight from the Explore page with the Upload a book button, which opens the same form without leaving the cards.

The kinds of writing you will find

Treeshelf has no single flavour. A reader might open a campus romance in the morning, a folktale retold for modern readers at lunch and a collection of poems at night. Typical genres include:

  • Short stories and flash fiction
  • Novels and novellas, in chapters or complete
  • Poetry and spoken-word collections
  • Romance, drama and family stories
  • Fantasy, science fiction and adventure
  • Mystery, thriller and horror
  • Memoir, personal essays and true stories
  • Children’s stories and bedtime tales
  • Faith, reflection and self-improvement writing

Why we keep it simple

Many platforms ask readers to sign up, follow, subscribe, unlock or pay before they read a single page. Treeshelf does the opposite. There are no pop-ups between you and the first paragraph, no coins to buy and no feeds to scroll. The site is a shelf. You walk up, pick a book and read it.

For writers, simplicity means you spend your time writing and not managing a profile. Your book is the page, and the page is the point.

Reading well, and sharing kindly

Amateur writing is often a very personal thing, and some of it is fragile. A few small habits keep the community welcoming for everyone:

  • Upload only work you wrote yourself or have permission to share. Copying another author’s book is not allowed.
  • Keep content suitable for a general audience. Mark mature themes clearly in the description so readers can choose.
  • If you tell an author what you think, tell them what worked first, then what could grow. Everyone here is learning.
  • Respect authors’ credit. If you share a book with a friend, share the name of its writer too.

Your words stay yours

Treeshelf does not claim ownership of anything you share. Your story, poem or book belongs to you, now and always. Showing it on this site is simply a way to let readers reach it. If you ever want a book removed, you can remove it from the Explore page yourself.

Questions people ask

Do I need an account to read?

No. Open Explore, pick a card and press Read.

Do I need an account to write?

Yes, a free writer account. It lets you publish and later remove your own books. Readers never need one.

Which file types can I upload?

PDF, Word (.docx) and plain text (.txt). Older .doc files can be uploaded and downloaded by readers, but they cannot be displayed inside the reader, so we recommend saving them as .docx or PDF first.

How big can my file be?

Up to 25 MB per book, which is enough for a very long novel or a book with plenty of images.

Is my book finished enough to share?

If you are proud enough of it to let one person read it, it is ready. Many writers share a chapter at a time.

Can I upload a book that is still a draft?

Yes. Just say so in the description, so readers know what to expect.
